Origins of Mathematics – Recommended Books

There is no required textbook for this course. The books below are recommended for exploring topics in greater depth, supplementing the lesson notes, and getting started on your research project.

General Mathematics and Its History

  • Math History: A Long-Form Mathematics Textbook, Jay Cummings.
    This is the book on which I base much of the course.
  • The History of Mathematics: An Introduction, David M. Burton.
  • The Saga of Mathematics: A Brief History, Marty Lewinter and William Widulski.
  • Journey Through Genius: The Great Theorems of Mathematics, William Dunham.

Mathematics, Art, and Culture

  • Mathematics and Art: A Cultural History, Lynn Gamwell.
  • The Golden Ratio: The Divine Beauty of Mathematics, Gary B. Meisner.
  • The Magic Mirror of M.C. Escher, Bruno Ernst.
  • The First Six Books of the Elements of Euclid, Oliver Byrne.
    A striking visual reinterpretation of Euclid's Elements.

Further Reading

  • The Music of the Primes, Marcus du Sautoy.
  • Alice's Adventures in Wonderland and Through the Looking-Glass, Lewis Carroll, SeaWolf Press Illustrated Classic.
    This is not a mistake—there is a lot of mathematics there!

Many of these books are available through libraries, including interlibrary loan. You are also welcome to borrow copies from me. Jay Cummings’ book is on reserve at the Purchase College Library.
