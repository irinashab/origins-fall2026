How Grading Works in Origins of Mathematics

This course uses a proficiency-based grading system for Problem Sets and Afterthoughts. The goal is to emphasize learning, revision, and demonstrated understanding rather than accumulating points.

Levels

Level 3: Proficient

You have demonstrated proficiency with the material. For Problem Sets, Level 3 represents A-range work.

Level 2: Progressing

You demonstrate good understanding of the material, but the work contains errors or needs some refinement. For Problem Sets, Level 2 represents B-range work.

Level 1: Developing

The work demonstrates some understanding, but has significant gaps, errors, or incomplete reasoning.

Level 0: No submission

Nothing was submitted for evaluation.

Nothing can come of nothing.

— William Shakespeare, King Lear, Act I, Scene I

Levels are not points. A Level 2 does not mean 2 out of 3, or 67%. The levels describe the proficiency demonstrated by your work. At the end of the semester, your work in each category will be considered as a whole and assigned a category grade.

Problem Sets — 25% of the Course Grade

Several substantial problem sets will be assigned during the semester. They may include calculations, proofs and mathematical arguments, historical methods, and conceptual questions.

Revisions

Level 2 work may be revised to demonstrate Level 3 proficiency.

  • Revise your work based on the feedback.
  • Submit the improved version within one week of receiving feedback.
  • Be prepared to explain your revisions if asked.

Level 1 work may also be revised.

  • Come to office hours or schedule a meeting with me to discuss the work.
  • Revise the assignment based on our discussion and the feedback.
  • Submit the improved version within one week of receiving feedback.

The level recorded after revision reflects the highest level of proficiency you ultimately demonstrate, not an average of your attempts.

At the end of the semester, the Problem Sets category will receive a grade based on your work as a whole. Consistent Level 3 work represents the A range; consistent Level 2 work represents the B range. A mixture of levels will be evaluated based on the overall pattern of your work, including improvement and demonstrated proficiency over time.

Afterthoughts — 10% of the Course Grade

Afterthoughts are short, low-stakes activities completed near the end of class. They are intended to show thoughtful engagement with the day's mathematics, not speed or perfect correctness.

  • Level 3 — Thoughtful: serious engagement with the question; reasoning is evident.
  • Level 2 — Partial: meaningful engagement, but the response is incomplete or underdeveloped.
  • Level 1 — Minimal: very limited attempt or little evidence of engagement.
  • Level 0 — No submission.

A mathematically incorrect answer can still receive Level 3 if it demonstrates serious and appropriate reasoning.

Afterthoughts cannot be revised or made up. Your three lowest Afterthought scores will be dropped. This provides flexibility for occasional absences, unfinished work, or simply having an off day.

After the three lowest scores are dropped, your remaining Afterthoughts will be considered as a whole and assigned a category grade.

A Note About Category Grades

Proficiency grading is intentionally different from averaging points. I am interested in the level of understanding you ultimately demonstrate.

For example, a student whose Problem Set levels develop from 1 → 2 → 2 → 3 → 3 has shown a different pattern of learning from a student whose work moves in the opposite direction. The final category grade may reflect that difference.

The purpose of revision is not to collect additional points. It is to learn the mathematics and demonstrate that you have learned it.
